# lockin — vision deck speaker notes

> **audience:** student builders, hackathon hackers, project creators, campus community organizers  
> **format:** 5 slides · 16:9 widescreen · high-restraint apple editorial style  
> **total delivery time:** 5–7 minutes + open builder discussion  
> **presenter mindset:** collegial, direct, zero startup jargon, pure founder conviction. you are not selling an app; you are inviting peers to build campus execution infrastructure with you.

---

## delivery principles

1. **do not rush the negative space.** this deck has generous dark margins and large typography. when you change slides, pause for a full beat (1 to 2 seconds) so the headline registers before you speak.
2. **all lowercase cadence.** speak in a relaxed, understated, confident rhythm. avoid loud motivational hype or generic startup pitch inflections.
3. **respect builder intelligence.** student builders know when they are being marketed to. do not inflate numbers, do not make synthetic claims, and do not pretend the product is finished. treat them as potential co-architects of campus culture.

## slide 02 · the problem

### visual anchor
- section tag: `● root cause diagnosis`
- headline: `good ideas rarely happen on their own.`
- 4 friction quadrants:
  - `01 / talk vs execution`
  - `02 / social isolation`
  - `03 / accountability vacuum`
  - `04 / coordination fatigue`
- summary bar: `the barrier isn't ambition: discovery, compatibility, and follow-through are fragmented across dead group chats, stale dms, and disconnected feeds. [ 0% output ]`

### delivery time
- 90 seconds

### spoken script & pacing

*(advance to slide 2. allow the four quadrants to settle on screen.)*

"when projects fail in college, people usually blame lack of discipline or lack of time. 

that's the wrong diagnosis. the problem isn't ambition. the problem is coordination friction. 

let's look at the four ways good intentions die every day on campus:

**first, talk versus execution.** everyone in this room has been in that group chat. someone has a brilliant idea for a hackathon or a campus app. twenty people join the whatsapp group. eighty-seven messages later, nobody has scaffolded a repository, nobody has written a line of code, and the project quietly disappears into notification silence.

**second, social isolation.** universities put thousands of people in massive lecture halls, but knowing fifty faces is not the same as having someone on your exact wavelength. you can sit next to someone for an entire semester and never know they're looking for a co-founder or a backend engineer.

**third, the accountability vacuum.** you promise yourself you'll spend sunday evening learning rust or studying fluid dynamics. you sit down in your room, open your laptop, and within twenty minutes you're trapped in an algorithmic feed. working alone in a dorm room is a battle against the best engagement algorithms on earth.

**and fourth, coordination fatigue.** you want to go for a 5k run, hit the gym, or study in the quiet zone of the library. but trying to coordinate that with friends requires five text messages, two maybes, one flake, and forty minutes of wasted energy. so you just end up staying in your room.

ambition is high. output is zero. because campus has zero execution infrastructure."

## slide 03 · the vision

### visual anchor
- lockin logo vector glyph centered
- master headline: `find your people.`
- subhead: `lockin helps you find things to do, people to do them with, and the momentum to actually get started.`
- comparison frame: `tinder × linkedin — but for college life.`
  - `01 / discovery without awkwardness`
  - `02 / alignment around ambition`
  - `03 / turning discovery into doing`

### delivery time
- 75 seconds

### spoken script & pacing

*(advance to slide 3. hold on the logo mark and the phrase 'find your people.')*

"this brings us to what we're building. 

our core thesis is simple: **find your people.**

college shouldn't feel like a disconnected feed. college needs an operating system for execution—something designed from the ground up to turn intent into real-world momentum.

if you look at consumer tech over the last decade, two products solved fundamental discovery problems:

**tinder** solved mutual intent. it removed the fear of rejection and the awkwardness of the cold approach. if two people want the same thing, the barrier disappears.

**linkedin** solved professional context. it anchored discovery around what people build, what skills they have, and where they want to go.

imagine synthesizing those two ideas specifically for university life:

from tinder, we take low-friction, zero-awkwardness discovery. you shouldn't have to cold dm ten strangers to find someone who wants to build an ai agent or study for a mid-term tonight.

from linkedin, we take the focus on capability, projects, and shared ambition. you connect around what you're actually doing.

**but here is the fundamental breakthrough:**

neither tinder nor linkedin gets people off their screens. both apps keep you trapped in endless swiping or endless feeds.

lockin is built on the opposite premise. discovery is just the spark. the goal of lockin is to get you into physical space, sitting across from a collaborator, running a synchronized timer, and shipping work."

## slide 04 · the product

### visual anchor
- headline: `from intention to action.`
- subhead: `the mission is the atomic unit of the product.`
- right badge: `● live on lockin.top`
- 3 authentic production ui screens:
  - `step 01 · discover` (real discovery feed with categories and problem sets)
  - `step 02 · do it solo or together` (mission launch with builder squad slots)
  - `step 03 · show up` (active sprint timer, attendance verification, proof log)

### delivery time
- 90 seconds

### spoken script & pacing

*(advance to slide 4. gesture toward the phone screens.)*

"this is not a figma mockup, and this is not a theoretical deck. lockin is live in production today on `lockin.top`.

the core atomic unit of lockin is the **mission**. everything revolves around it in three simple steps:

**step one: discover.** 
you open the platform and see real missions happening across campus right now. they're categorized by track: grind, study, fitness, and discipline. you might see a 60-minute problem set sprint at the central library, a hackathon build squad forming in hostel 7, or a 5k paced campus run. you don't scroll an algorithmic feed—you browse concrete opportunities to execute.

**step two: solo or squad.** 
lockin respects both solo focus and collaborative builds. if you need to lock in alone to write code or study without distraction, you launch an independent mission. if you need teammates, you open confirmed roster slots. builders can request a slot, the host accepts, and everyone commits to a specific place and time.

**step three: show up & stack grit.** 
this is where the loop closes. when you arrive on-site, you verify attendance. a synchronized focus timer starts. notifications and distractions are silenced. you check off your deliverables, and when the mission finishes, you submit tangible proof of work.

you earn **grit**—lockin's reputation metric for verified execution. on lockin, your standing isn't based on vanity follower counts, aesthetics, or how many connections you collected. it is based entirely on how often you actually showed up and shipped."

## builder q&a playbook

### question 1: "why wouldn't people just use a whatsapp group or a discord server?"
> **answer:** "whatsapp and discord are messaging tools, not commitment mechanisms. in a group chat, the social cost of agreeing to a plan and then flaking is almost zero. because chats have no structure, no scheduled check-in, no timer, and no proof-of-work history, 90% of plans die in conversation. lockin is built around the mission, not the chat. it replaces open-ended chatting with discrete slots, synchronized sprint timers, and verified grit."

### question 2: "what stops someone from faking their work or cheating the grit score?"
> **answer:** "first, lockin requires on-site attendance verification and host confirmation for group sessions. second, the grit system is built on verifiable proof of work (links to pull requests, deployed projects, completed problem sets, or photo check-ins). we are actively building peer-reviewed verification where teammates confirm milestone completion. fake participation destroys value, which is why our architecture prioritizes verified offline execution over superficial clicks."

### question 3: "what if nobody on my campus is using it yet? the cold start problem."
> **answer:** "that's why lockin works solo first. unlike purely social apps that are useless with zero friends, lockin is immediately useful as an individual focus and sprint tool. you can use it tonight to lock in for two hours of deep work, track your streak, and build your grit score. then you invite one friend to do a joint study block. we grow campus by campus, lab by lab, hostel by hostel—anchoring on high-density builder squads rather than broad, shallow ads."

### question 4: "are you going to turn this into a corporate recruiter tool or an ad network?"
> **answer:** "no. college students already have linkedin for polished resumes and instagram for algorithmic distraction. lockin's integrity rests on being a dark-mode, high-restraint execution space. if monetization happens in the future, it will be around verified builder credentials, premium hackathon tooling, and campus partnership challenges—never intrusive banner ads or selling student attention."
